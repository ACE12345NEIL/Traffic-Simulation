# Master Prompt: T25 — Improved Cellular Automaton Model for Mumbai Traffic

Act as a Python developer and simulation-modelling researcher. Build a complete, simple, functional traffic simulation with a FastAPI web interface and a research report in Markdown.

Do not merely reproduce the benchmark paper. The project must implement the original model as a baseline, develop a meaningful extension for Mumbai mixed traffic, and evaluate whether the extension improves the representation of urban traffic behaviour.

Prioritise simplicity, correctness, explainability, and reproducible results. Avoid unnecessary features and complex architecture.

# 1. Project Details

- **Topic ID:** T25
- **Title:** Cellular-Automaton Traffic Simulation of a Congested Mumbai Arterial Road
- **Model:** Simulation and Stochastic Modelling
- **Research question:** How do lane discipline, random slowdowns and signal timing affect flow and travel time on a congested road, and can the model reproduce the observed fundamental diagram?
- **Benchmark paper:** K. Nagel and M. Schreckenberg, “A cellular automaton model for freeway traffic,” Journal de Physique I, vol. 2, no. 12, pp. 2221–2229, 1992.
- **Research contribution:** Extend the original Nagel–Schreckenberg (NaSch) model to represent heterogeneous Indian traffic, particularly cars, two-wheelers, lane discipline and traffic signals.

## 2. Main Deliverables

# STAGE 2 — Reproduce the Original NaSch Model

Implement the original single-lane Nagel–Schreckenberg model first, without Mumbai-specific extensions.

Represent the road as discrete cells. Each cell is either empty or occupied by a vehicle. Each vehicle has an integer velocity from zero to a configurable maximum.

Apply these four rules in the correct order:

1. **Acceleration:** If velocity is below the maximum, increase it by one.
2. **Braking:** If the gap ahead is insufficient, reduce velocity to avoid collision.
3. **Randomisation:** With probability `p`, reduce a positive velocity by one.
4. **Movement:** Move every vehicle forward by its updated velocity.

Use synchronous updates. Calculate all new velocities before moving any vehicle.

Use a circular road for the baseline fundamental-diagram experiment. Vehicles that cross the end re-enter at the beginning.

Use a fixed random seed so that experiments can be reproduced.

Requirements:
- Test all four update rules.
- Verify that vehicles do not overlap.
- Calculate density, mean speed and flow.
- Include a warm-up period before measuring steady-state behaviour.
- Generate the baseline flow-versus-density graph.

Document any implementation details that differ from the benchmark paper.

**Do not modify the original model to make it resemble Mumbai.** Keep it as an independent baseline for comparison.

# STAGE 3 — Develop the Improved Mumbai Model

Create a separate extended model that reuses the validated baseline logic wherever appropriate.

The extended model must include three additions.

## Extension A: Heterogeneous vehicles

Represent:
- Cars
- Two-wheelers

Give each vehicle type configurable parameters, including maximum speed and random slowdown probability.

For the initial implementation, both types may occupy one cell. Explain that this is a computational simplification and does not mean the physical vehicles have equal dimensions.

Keep total road occupancy and the proportion of each vehicle type configurable.

Investigate whether changing the two-wheeler share affects flow, speed, travel time and congestion.

Do not assume in advance that a higher two-wheeler share necessarily improves traffic flow.

## Extension B: Lane discipline and safe lane changing

Extend the model to two lanes.

Introduce a lane-discipline parameter that influences how frequently a vehicle attempts a lane change when another lane offers an advantage.

Support three settings:
- Strong lane discipline
- Moderate lane discipline
- Weak lane discipline

Implement safe lane-changing rules:
- The target cell must be free.
- The target lane must have sufficient space ahead.
- The manoeuvre must not cause an immediate collision with a vehicle behind.
- Lane changes must be performed consistently so that two vehicles cannot claim the same cell.

If vehicle types use different lane-changing thresholds or gap preferences, document these explicitly as modelling assumptions.

Start with simple rules. Do not attempt to reproduce every detail of real Indian driving behaviour.

## Extension C: Signal-controlled traffic

Add one signalised stop line on the road.

Implement:
- Configurable green duration
- Configurable red duration
- Signal cycle
- A stop-line constraint
- Queue formation and dissipation

Vehicles must stop before the red signal and must never cross the stop line while the signal is red.

Use a simple, documented signal phase convention.

For the signalised model, use an open-road or finite-road configuration where vehicles enter and exit the simulated segment. This is more suitable for measuring completed trips and travel time than a circular road.

Use a consistent entry rule and record vehicles that enter, exit or remain in the road at the end of a run.

Keep the circular baseline and the signalised model separate where their boundary conditions differ.

# STAGE 7 — Numerical Experiments

Run all experiments systematically. Change one main factor at a time while keeping other parameters fixed, unless the experiment explicitly investigates combined effects.

Use multiple random seeds for stochastic comparisons. Report averages and variability.

## Experiment 1: Baseline fundamental diagram

For the original NaSch model:
- Vary density from low to high.
- Measure steady-state flow at each density.
- Plot flow against density.
- Explain free-flow and congested regimes.
- Identify the observed peak-flow region in the simulation.

Compare the qualitative shape with the benchmark paper. Do not assume that the exact density of the transition in the paper must occur with different parameter choices or road configurations.

## Experiment 2: Extended fundamental diagram

Generate the corresponding graph for the extended model.

State how flow is measured, particularly for an open road with a signal.

Discuss how mixed traffic, lane changing and signal interruptions alter the curve.

For an open-road system, report both throughput and density using a clearly defined measurement region. Do not treat the open-road and circular-road measurements as identical without explanation.

## Experiment 3: Random slowdowns

Compare low, medium and high random slowdown probabilities.

Measure flow, speed, travel time and queue formation.

## Experiment 4: Lane discipline

Compare strong, moderate and weak lane discipline in the two-lane model.

Measure flow, travel time and queue length.

Discuss whether more lane changing consistently helps, or whether the outcome depends on density and vehicle composition.

## Experiment 5: Signal timing

Compare at least three green/red splits while keeping total cycle length fixed.

Measure throughput, average travel time, average queue length and maximum queue length.

Explain the trade-offs between green allocation, red-phase waiting and queue clearance.

## Experiment 6: Mixed traffic

Explain which quantities remain fixed and why.

# STAGE 8 — Stability and Sensitivity Analysis

Conduct a numerical stability analysis, not an unsupported claim of mathematical proof.

## A. Disturbance propagation

Create an initially moving traffic stream. Introduce a small disturbance, such as one vehicle slowing down.

Track vehicle speeds, gaps or queue length over time.

Repeat at different densities and random slowdown probabilities.

Classify the outcome as:
- Disturbance dissipates
- Disturbance remains limited
- Disturbance grows into a traffic jam

Repeat this test for the original and extended models where a meaningful comparison is possible.

## B. Parameter sensitivity

Vary one parameter at a time, such as slowdown probability, two-wheeler share or green-time proportion.

Measure the resulting percentage change in mean flow and travel time.

Show results in tables and graphs.

## C. Stochastic repeatability

Run identical configurations with multiple random seeds.

Report the mean and standard deviation of the outputs.

Explain:
- Computational correctness
- Stochastic variability
- Physical traffic stability

Do not call a model physically stable simply because the code runs without errors.
